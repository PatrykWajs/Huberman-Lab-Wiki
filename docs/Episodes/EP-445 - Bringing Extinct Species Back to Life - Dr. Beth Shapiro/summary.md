# Bringing Extinct Species Back to Life | Dr. Beth Shapiro

## Key Findings

- Deextinction relies on engineering specific genetic changes from extinct species into the genomes of living relatives, using genome sequencing and gene editing. — *[00:00:00](transcript.md#000000-slug)*
- The same biotechnological tools used for deextinction also support conservation efforts for living endangered species. — *[00:00:28](transcript.md#000028-slug)*
- Species concepts, like those based on breeding or genetics, are human-made frameworks and don't always match biological reality. — *[00:04:51](transcript.md#000451-slug)*
- Taxonomy frequently reflects historical naming, not genetic relationships, e.g., "buffalo" refers to several unrelated species. — *[00:05:00](transcript.md#000500-slug)*
- Genomics has shown that many extinct human relatives (like Neanderthals and Denisovans) interbred with Homo sapiens, indicating permeable species boundaries. — *[00:10:00](transcript.md#001000-slug)*
- Most people today have between 2% and 5% Neanderthal DNA, but the specific Neanderthal segments differ between individuals. — *[00:23:01](transcript.md#002301-slug)*
- Ancient DNA research has narrowed down the specific genetic differences that make modern humans unique compared to Neanderthals. — *[00:25:18](transcript.md#002518-slug)*
- Some Neanderthal gene variants in modern humans have health and disease implications, such as immune function and COVID-19 risk. — *[00:27:00](transcript.md#002700-slug)*
- Technical limitations prevent the deextinction of dinosaurs due to the lack of recoverable DNA; oldest DNA samples are from mammoths (~1–2 million years old). — *[00:33:01](transcript.md#003301-slug)*
- The selection of species for deextinction is driven by technical feasibility, ecological necessity, ethical considerations, and public engagement. — *[00:32:34](transcript.md#003234-slug)*
- Reintroducing keystone species like mammoths could restore and stabilize ecosystems, such as creating plant diversity in Siberian tundra. — *[00:36:21](transcript.md#003621-slug)*
- Modern synthetic biology and cell engineering developed for deextinction are directly applicable to helping endangered birds and mammals. — *[00:40:01](transcript.md#004001-slug)*
- Successful avian deextinction is technically challenging since standard cloning is not possible for birds due to their reproductive biology. — *[00:40:46](transcript.md#004046-slug)*
- Public excitement and the sense of awe around iconic extinct species (like dodos and mammoths) drive investment and innovation in conservation technology. — *[00:41:25](transcript.md#004125-slug)*
- Rare reproductive events, such as parthenogenesis in birds, reveal unique aspects of developmental genetics with conservation implications. — *[00:43:05](transcript.md#004305-slug)*

## Tools & Protocols

### Ancient DNA Sequencing & Genome Engineering
- **What:** Extracting and sequencing DNA from extinct species' fossils; editing genomes of living relatives to introduce extinct traits.
- **How:** Identify viable, well-preserved DNA; compare with living species; use CRISPR and other gene editing to recreate desired traits.
- **When:** Applied to projects involving mammoths, direwolves, and dodos.
- **Ref:** [00:00:00](transcript.md#000000-slug), [00:33:01](transcript.md#003301-slug)

### Synthetic Biology for Conservation
- **What:** Applying genetic engineering, iPS cell technologies, and multiplex genome engineering to conservation and preventing extinction.
- **How:** Develop a toolkit for modifying the genomes of endangered species to enhance resilience or restore lost diversity.
- **When:** Used in both deextinction and protection of living species.
- **Ref:** [00:40:01](transcript.md#004001-slug)
